This is the opening paragraph — answer-first. State the thesis in the first 200 words. No throat-clearing. The reader should know whether to keep reading by the end of this paragraph.

A second paragraph that frames the *why now*. What changed? What's the trigger event — a paper, a launch, a market shift — that makes this worth 3,000 words today instead of last quarter or next quarter.

## The shift
Establish the change. What is the world like *now* that wasn't the case before. Be specific — name the system, the cost curve, the technique, the model.

## Why it matters
Translate the shift into stakes. For whom does it matter, and how. Avoid generic "this could be transformative" framing — name the operator who has to do something different on Monday.

## What's actually new
Distinguish hype from substance. The ten-paragraph section that earns the post's existence. Specific examples, numbers, mechanisms. Cite the source.

## What I'd watch
Forward-looking, but specific. Not predictions — *signals*. "If X happens by Q3, the thesis holds. If Y happens, the thesis breaks."

## How I'd build for this
The case-study turn — what would you do differently if you were starting today. Concrete. This is the section that proves you've thought about it, not just read about it.
